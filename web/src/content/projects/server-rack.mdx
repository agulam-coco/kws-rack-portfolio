This is the rack this whole site is based on. It sits on my desk in my dorm room.

![The finished rack on my desk](/media/projects/server-rack/hero.jpg)

## Why I built it

I started this in August because I wanted to stop paying for iCloud. My photos were my main thing, and I figured, why rent storage when I could just own it?

A friend lent me a few Raspberry Pis and some HATs, so I had somewhere to start. I ordered some hard drives off eBay and started learning as I went: Tailscale, SMB file shares, and a lot of Linux stuff I'd never touched before.

## Before the rack

Before this, my "backup system" was two hard drives sitting in a dock on my desk. Every now and then I'd plug the dock into my laptop and copy stuff over. If I forgot, nothing got backed up.

Then I got the Pis and wired everything up on my desk to test it before building anything nicer. That worked, but it was a mess of cables and loose drives.

## Printing the rack

Then I found the [KWS Rack v2](https://makerworld.com/en/models/2139130), a 10 inch homelab rack you can 3D print, designed by Ilan Kushnir. I didn't design any of the rack myself, so all the credit for the design goes to him. I just printed it and put it together.

I printed all the parts at the [Stew Makerspace](https://www.stew.sites.grinnell.edu) in Grinnell. The rails came out in black and the frame in white.

I'd never really 3D printed anything before this, so huge thanks to Colby at the Stew. He helped me with pretty much everything on the printer side, from infill settings to getting prints to actually come out right, and helped me put the whole thing together. I wouldn't have gotten this far without him 🙏

![The Bambu Lab printer at the Stew](/media/projects/server-rack/printer.jpg)

*Most of the rack came off this printer.*

![A batch of printed rails](/media/projects/server-rack/rails.jpg)

The rails need brass heat set inserts so the screws have something to bite into. I ordered the inserts and nuts off AliExpress and spent a good chunk of time at the Stew melting them in one by one with a soldering iron. It's weirdly satisfying 🔥

## What's running on it

Right now there's a network switch up top and a row of Pi sleds under it. Two of the Pis do the real work:

- **The tunnel.** This is how I get to everything from anywhere, using Tailscale.
- **The NAS.** It holds the drives and handles file sharing over SMB, so I can open my files from my laptop like they're local.

My photos back up with [Immich](https://immich.app). Now everything just backs up on its own to the rack in my room. No more plugging drives in.

The switch sits on its own printed shelf, with a honeycomb top so it can breathe.

## What's next

I'm still making it more reliable. The goal is to follow the 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies of my stuff, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 somewhere other than my room. Once that's done I can finally cancel iCloud. Soon.
